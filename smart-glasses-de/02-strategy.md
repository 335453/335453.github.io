# Strategy & Go-to-Market — WERKBLICK (Working Title), Germany

**Newagent / Strategy stage | 20 November 2026**  
**Derived from:** `01-research.md` | **Status:** Hypothesis-led business concept; not a confirmed product, vendor partnership, or operating company.

## 1. Positioning

> **WERKBLICK helps German midmarket manufacturers turn one difficult frontline task into a hands-free, measurable digital workflow on existing industrial smart glasses — with employee privacy considered from day one.**

**Category:** Workflow design, device-compatible implementation and change management for industrial assisted-reality pilots; **not** a new smart-glasses manufacturer.  
**Core job:** Make work instructions accessible at the point of execution, verify work completion where operationally appropriate, and show quantified process impact against a baseline.  
**Brand promise:** *Arbeit im Blick. Hände frei.* ("Work in sight. Hands free.")  
**Important qualification:** WERKBLICK is a provisional concept name; no trademark, domain or device compatibility clearance has been completed.

## 2. Target segments and use cases

### Beachhead: German industrial SMEs with recurring manual inspection / quality-check routines
- **Budget owner:** Operations, quality or plant director; IT/security and worker representatives are approval stakeholders.
- **Pain hypothesis:** SOPs on paper/tablets pull attention away from the workpiece; variability between workers causes repeat work or missed checks.
- **Pilot:** One non-safety-critical recurring routine such as end-of-line visual quality checklist, incoming-goods verification or scheduled equipment inspection.
- **Success metrics:** Median completion time per case; first-pass checklist completion; corrective rework; worker comfort/usability; optional completed check-record quality. Record safety incidents separately. No promise of impact before measurement.
- **Explicit exclusion:** Do not make the system the sole safety control, monitor individuals' performance, or deploy cameras in sensitive spaces without suitable legal approvals.

### Secondary: Maintenance and field-service teams
- **Buyer:** Maintenance manager; **pilot:** guided standard maintenance checklist for one machine family / recurring job.
- **Defer remote-video assistance** until permissions, use-case justification, employee consent/consultation if relevant and privacy assessment are completed.

**Not now:** Consumer retail smart glasses, real-time video surveillance, custom optical hardware, generalized enterprise AR platforms, complex live SAP/MES integration, clinical environments.

## 3. Differentiation versus incumbents

| Choice | Incumbent alternative | Proposed narrow distinction | Must be validated |
|---|---|---|---|
| General enterprise AR rollout | TeamViewer Frontline and systems integrators | Fixed-scope 4–6 week *single-process* pilot, followed by a measured go/no-go report | Buyer values standardized package over existing provider |
| Hardware-first project | RealWear and device resellers | Hardware selection / loan via partners plus process redesign and operator enablement | Specific model/API/MDM compatibility and partner terms |
| Technology-led innovation theater | Ad hoc proof-of-concept | Before/after baseline with 3–4 operational metrics and worker feedback | Credibility and acceptable measurement design |
| Unclear recording policies | Some ad hoc deployments | Design goal: camera off by default; no video storage in initial inspection pilot; clear privacy review and worker engagement | Legal assessment confirms acceptable data flows |

**Not defensible claims:** "Germany's first," "100% GDPR compliant," "works with every device," "guaranteed 30% efficiency gains," "secure EU hosting" (unless implemented and audited).

## 4. Minimum viable offer

**Six-week guided pilot** (the package itself is a proposal, not currently available):
1. **Week 0–1:** Use-case discovery, stakeholder map, safety/IT/privacy screening, employee involvement, on-site baseline and photo-free process map.
2. **Week 2:** Select one supported industrial device and one workflow. Confirm display/voice control, protective equipment, local network, and no unnecessary collection. Create work instruction templates.
3. **Week 3–4:** In controlled conditions, run workflow with 5–10 volunteer users and a like-for-like comparison process. Offer training and a fallback paper/tablet SOP.
4. **Week 5:** Measure quantitative and qualitative results, error modes, adoption, fatigue/discomfort, safe operation and data-protection issues.
5. **Week 6:** Deliver a business case, evidence/limitations, risk list, architecture options and scale/no-go decision.

**Demo feature set (proposed, unbuilt):** step-by-step checklists, visual reference cues, voice/gesture next-step if device supports it, pause / back / report exception, optional local audit event record with governance policy. Device interface integrations are not yet built.

**Pricing:** Test a **EUR 5,000–10,000 ex-VAT fixed professional-services pilot** in customer interviews as a willingness-to-pay hypothesis only, with hardware rental and travel separately itemized. No assertion of pricing acceptance or positive unit economics. If participants will not pay, test a smaller funded discovery engagement or narrow scope.

## 5. Go-to-market sequence — first 90 days

| Phase | Actions | Evidence gate |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–21: problem discovery | 20 interviews: 8 operations/quality, 5 maintenance, 4 IT/security/works council, 3 device integrators; shadow 3 real workflows; competitor quotation requests | Repeated painful workflow exists; minimum 5 serious buyers name problem with quantified cost |
| Days 22–40: feasibility | Contact 2 device/solution partners; validate **one** device/software pairing; build a no-recording clickable workflow demo; have legal/privacy and worker-safety review | Can demonstrate selected workflow without prohibited monitoring or critical safety gaps |
| Days 41–65: commercial experiment | 25 targeted account outreach contacts via IHK/manufacturing clusters and direct referrals; 6 qualified discovery meetings; 2 scoped proposals | **One signed paid pilot** or clear kill/pivot insight; these are *targets*, not forecasts |
| Days 66–90: pilot and decision | Instrument baseline, operate one controlled pilot if signed, document operator interviews, produce reference case only with customer consent | Explicit go / pivot / stop using customer evidence and unit economics |

**Channel plan:** owner-operated direct outreach to midmarket plant leaders; referrals via German chambers of industry and commerce, cluster networks and specialist integrators; demonstrate with a short German-language workflow video and site-specific ROI worksheet. Do not claim a channel agreement without one.

**Buyer journey:** problem assessment → on-site process mapping → security/worker-review → paid pilot → documented evaluation → rollout/decline. **Sales collateral:** German one-page factsheet, sample process flow, concise privacy/data-flow sheet, cost-of-rework calculator, clearly bounded pilot terms.

## 6. KPI scoreboard and go/no-go criteria

**Commercial (research stage):** 20 interviews, 3 observed processes, 6 buyer discovery meetings, 2 proposals, 1 paid pilot sought. **These are chosen validation milestones, not expected conversion rates.**  
**Operational (pilot):** collect 20+ comparable samples per process route if feasible; compare baseline and pilot medians, completion errors and exception rates with controls for operator experience/mix. Report confidence and limitations.  
**Trust and adoption:** zero unresolved severe privacy/safety incidents; legal/works council review completed where required; average usability rating from staff; safe fallback functioning.  
**Commercial scale decision:** positive contribution after deployment labor, device/partner and support costs; unambiguous operational benefit; customer willingness to buy; reproducibility on a second site.

### Stop/pivot signals
- No buyer can value a problem sufficiently to fund a discovery or pilot after 20 conversations.
- Device compatibility or procurement defeats fixed-scope economics.
- Privacy/works council/safety review disallows the use case.
- Pilot does not outperform a well-designed non-glasses tablet or voice-only workflow under comparable conditions.

## 7. Risk register

- **Strong competitors:** TeamViewer already proves AR use cases and integrates enterprise workflows. **Response:** test a one-process SME offer; do not claim exclusive advantage.
- **Small service margin:** device support and customization can consume paid pilot fees. **Response:** track consultant hours, support, training, travel and failed trials.
- **Worker resistance:** users may perceive surveillance. **Response:** staff co-design, clear camera behavior, no individual performance ranking, independent privacy review.
- **Technology lock-in:** device APIs may constrain the product. **Response:** start with one supported pairing, do not market universality.
- **Commercial timing:** industrial procurement may exceed 90 days. **Response:** record stages separately and judge willingness to pay rather than arbitrary close dates.

## 8. Strategic recommendation

**GO for discovery / NO-GO for immediate full product launch.** Validate whether German SMEs will pay for an objectively measured, privacy-conscious, narrow smart-glasses deployment service. Scale only if the first repeatable pilot beats a simpler non-glasses alternative and survives legal, ergonomic and unit-economics reviews.
