One-hand sign guide & story notes
Tiger-inspired half-seal

Index and middle finger upright and touching; fold ring and little finger. A sword-finger / confrontation-style pose. Great Fireball in this game.

Dragon-inspired half-seal

Extend thumb and little finger. Fold index, middle and ring finger. Keep the thumb clearly separated. Water Dragon in this game.

Ox-inspired half-seal

Raise index and little finger; fold the middle two. Tuck the thumb across them. Chakra Cloak in this game.

Most Naruto animal seals use two hands. These half-seals and jutsu mappings are simplified one-hand game controls, not exact canonical casting sequences. Rasengan needs no hand seals in the story; Amaterasu is an eye technique. The two-hand Shadow Clone control is also simplified.

How to train & play
  • Practice: hold a sign for 0.2 seconds to morph the dots into its jutsu, then keep that sign visible. Dots disperse when the pose is unrecognized or the hand leaves the camera. Speed and closeness shape the active jutsu.
  • Speed adds sparks and rotation. Closeness increases the aura size and intensity. It is a relative estimate from palm size, not a distance measurement.
  • Match: fist beats V, V beats palm, palm beats fist. First to 3 wins. Hold for 0.6 seconds; lower both hands out of frame before the next round. Power changes the visuals; the match uses standard rock-paper-scissors rules.
  • Offline, buttons or keys 1–9 simulate a sign (1–3 in a match); use Release / scatter to disperse the dots. With the camera on, formation requires a recognized hand sign. The extra techniques are for practice. Toggle Classic mode for plain match names.
  • Tiger and Dragon are one-hand adaptations; see the sign guide for finger placement and story notes. Use good lighting, separate the fingers, and face your palm toward the camera.
